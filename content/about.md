Hello. I should probably begin with the most important part of introducing myself: my first and last name. Since neither is something we get to choose, I consider myself lucky to have been quite happy with both of mine so far. I am therefore very happy to claim them as my own: Alp Yağcıoğlu.

I spent much of my life shuttling between Datça and Denizli in Turkey’s Aegean region. As a result, I grew up within Mediterranean culture and, more specifically, its Aegean branch. This meant growing up among the remains of Mediterranean civilizations and carrying some of the Mediterranean warmth with me, although my grandfather has always said that I am a little reserved.

Istanbul and Turin have since been added to this route, though I now spend most of my time in Turin. From what I have observed here, I am slightly warmer than the average Turinese person. On Italy’s north-south spectrum, I suppose that makes me Florentine. At least, that is one way of calculating it.

Generally speaking, I am in favour of reading and writing, and I hope I am reasonably good at both. What I read and write usually wanders between history, philosophy, and the fundamental sciences. I do not mind when any of these subjects drift into fiction, whether in a romantic or didactic form. In fact, I find it immensely enjoyable.

I am also deeply interested in the visual arts, particularly architecture and visual storytelling. I had a natural inclination towards the social sciences before attending a science high school. Studying there and later at Politecnico di Torino, combined with the dialectical materialist outlook I already had, gradually turned me into someone who is shamelessly engineering-brained. I am quite proud of that.

At this point in my life, while trying to rethink and formalise the philosophy of language, I realised that the instrument I was using was the brain itself. Since then, I have turned all my attention towards the brain.
